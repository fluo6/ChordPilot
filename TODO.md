## How to maintain this file

- Keep stable item IDs. Mark completed items `[x]`, record the date and link the implementation or validation evidence.
- A deferred item is not an instruction to start it. **Rootless voicings and ML experiments are deferred at the user's request.**
- For recognition changes, record the concrete passage, settings, expected result, before/after result and regressions checked. Do not count a new formula as validated audio recognition.
- Track root, bass/inversion, harmonic function, exact extensions, timing and voicing-label precision separately. Keep user corrections separate from automatic predictions.
- Prefer shared fixes over song-specific rules. Reuse existing stems when testing chord changes; separate stem quality from recognition quality.

## Current limitations to remember

- Chroma/spectral evidence includes overtones, melody and separation leakage. A strong spectral peak or high periodicity score does not prove that a note was played as part of the chord.
- Complete registered half-diminished shells can receive bounded support against harmonic-looking major thirds when Overtone Weighting is enabled. Root/third octave support is required. A major third inside the voicing additionally needs measured roots one and two octaves below, tight fifth-partial frequency matching and a minor third at least 3 dB stronger. Conflicting bass, missing roots or stronger/detuned major thirds block it. This does not prove simultaneity or distinguish every independently played upper note from a coincident partial; weak/noisy cases can still fail.
- Local bass noise calibration now uses the whole recording in the same analysis bandwidth as full tracking. The per-frame amplitude gate can still omit very faint real notes or admit louder artifacts; it is not a general musical-relevance classifier.
- Sustain-tail reduction uses an assumed exponential power decay and adjustable half-life. Overtone weighting uses measured harmonic-family heuristics. Neither reconstructs the instrument's actual envelopes or perfectly separates overlapping notes.
- Arpeggio consolidation can hide brief changes if overly strong. Local Study does not apply the timeline's grid/stability pass, so results can still differ from grouped events. Both use early evidence inside eligible 0.5–2 s intervals to retain a fading measured root; strong new tones, clear low-note guidance or a conflicting measured third (≥6%, outside the formula) block the preference. That threshold is a heuristic and can abstain on melody/leakage. This is not a general onset detector or rootless-chord inference.
- Bass notes can be passing tones or inversions. A prominent bass note is not automatically the harmonic root; note-interior checks do not validate all attack/release times.
- Voicing subtitles currently cover **quartal · So What**, strict quartal stacks, drop-2 and drop-3 patterns only. They are optional **Possible …** labels and do not change chord ranking/confidence. They describe spacing, not confirmed fingering, hand allocation or artist style.
- Voicing detection requires stable octave-specific evidence: 320 ms snapshots, a shorter 40 ms persistence check, at least two supporting snapshots and 60% support for the same shape/register. Very short events, weak notes, dense arrangements, changing registers and extra doublings may yield no label. The persistence check is a heuristic, not proof of simultaneity.
- Legacy runs without the new evidence remain unlabeled until reanalysis. Current cache versions are `sub-bar-v23` and `local-study-v28`; saved results are not silently rewritten. Reanalyse Chords can reuse available stems.
- Reference videos and manually sampled highlights have timing/spelling uncertainty. Current validation covers selected windows, not complete authoritative song transcriptions. Synthetic voicing/layout fixtures are not real-recording accuracy demonstrations.
- Optional analysis dependencies and fallback/draft behavior need to remain clearly distinguished from successful audio recognition.
